Identifier expressions (C++ only)
An identifier expression, or id-expression,
is a restricted form of primary expression. Syntactically, an id-expression requires a higher level of complexity
than a simple identifier to provide a name for all of the language
elements of C++.
An id-expression can be either a qualified
or unqualified identifier. It can also appear after the dot and arrow
operators.

Identifier expression syntax

>>-+-| unqualified_id |-+--------------------------------------><
'-| qualified_id |---'

unqualified_id:

|--+-identifier-------------+-----------------------------------|
+-operator_function_id---+
+-conversion_function_id-+
+-~--class_name----------+
'-template_id------------'

qualified_id:

|--+-::--+-identifier-----------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------+--|
| +-operator_function_id-+ |
| '-template_id----------' |
| .------------------------------------------. |
| V | |
'-+----+----+--------------------------------------+-+--class_or_namespace--::--+----------+--unqualified_id-'
'-::-' '-class_or_namespace--::--+----------+-' '-template-'
'-template-'
